SBAR nursing report: template, example and a free printable

Updated 2026-10-25 · CalmShift by KitWise Studio

SBAR stands for Situation, Background, Assessment and Recommendation. It is a fixed order for telling another clinician what is happening with a patient, so the key facts and your request come first. Nurses use it to call providers and to give shift report. You can download a free printable SBAR sheet.

Why a structure matters

Handoffs are a known risk point. In 2010 the Joint Commission Center for Transforming Healthcare estimated that 80% of serious medical errors involve miscommunication between caregivers during handoffs. A shared structure like SBAR (or the I-SBAR variant developed by Kaiser Permanente of Colorado) makes it harder to leave out a vital fact.

The four parts

PartWhat to say
SituationWho you are, the patient, and the problem right now.
BackgroundAdmitting diagnosis, relevant history, current treatment, code status, allergies.
AssessmentLatest vitals and findings, and what you think is going on.
RecommendationWhat you need: an order, an evaluation, a time frame.

Example: calling a provider

S: This is the nurse on 4 West about Mr. B in room 12. His blood pressure is 86/50, down from 118/70 two hours ago.
B: He is post-op day 1 after a hip replacement, on scheduled oxycodone, no cardiac history.
A: HR 112, alert, urine output 20 mL/h for the last 2 hours. I am concerned about hypovolemia or bleeding.
R: Can you come and evaluate him? Would you like a fluid bolus and a CBC now?

Illustrative example only. Follow your facility's protocols.

Quick answers

What does SBAR stand for in nursing?

Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation.

Is there a free SBAR template?

Yes. CalmShift's SBAR Nursing Report Sheet with provider call notes is free to download on Gumroad, in printable and fillable PDF.

What is I-SBAR?

I-SBAR adds Identify (yourself and the patient) before the SBAR steps. Kaiser Permanente of Colorado developed it.
