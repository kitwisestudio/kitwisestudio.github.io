What is a nurse brain sheet, and which one should you use?

Updated 2026-10-27 · CalmShift by KitWise Studio

A nurse brain sheet (also called a report sheet) is a paper or PDF organizer where a nurse writes key facts for each assigned patient: diagnosis, vitals, lines, meds and times, labs, and to-dos. The right one depends on your patient load: ICU nurses usually need a 1 to 2 patient systems-based sheet, while med-surg nurses need a 4 to 6 patient sheet with a timeline.

What every brain sheet should include

  1. Patient basics: room, age, code status, allergies, isolation.
  2. Reason for admission and relevant history.
  3. Systems assessment (neuro, cardio, resp, GI/GU, skin) or a head-to-toe block.
  4. Lines, drains and drips.
  5. Medication times and an hourly timeline for the shift.
  6. Labs and pending tests.
  7. An SBAR block so the end-of-shift report follows the same order.

Paper or fillable?

Paper is fastest at the bedside. A fillable PDF is useful if you prep on a tablet or want clean copies. CalmShift sheets work both ways.

Quick answers

What is the difference between a brain sheet and a report sheet?

They are the same tool. Brain sheet and report sheet are both names for the page a nurse uses to organize patient information during a shift and to give handoff report.

What is the best brain sheet for new grad nurses?

One that matches your unit's patient load and includes an hourly timeline and an SBAR section, so you build a routine for assessments, meds and report. A kit with several layouts helps while you find your style.

Can I use a brain sheet with patient information?

Follow your facility's privacy policy. Many units require brain sheets to stay at work and be shredded at the end of the shift.
