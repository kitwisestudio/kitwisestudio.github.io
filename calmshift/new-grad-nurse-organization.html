How new grad nurses stay organized on a shift

Updated 2026-10-27 · CalmShift by KitWise Studio

New grad nurses stay organized by using one brain sheet per shift with an hourly timeline: write the facts during report, block out scheduled meds and assessments by hour, and give report back in SBAR order. A consistent sheet turns a chaotic shift into a checklist.

A routine that works

  1. Before report: print or open your sheet and fill names, rooms and code status.
  2. During report: follow the sheet's order so nothing is skipped.
  3. First hour: quick safety rounds, then fill the hourly timeline with med times, assessments, labs and procedures.
  4. Every hour: tick what's done and move what slipped.
  5. End of shift: give report in SBAR order straight from the sheet.

What to have

  • A brain sheet that fits your unit's patient load.
  • An SBAR block or sheet for provider calls.
  • A shift timeline.

Quick answers

What should a new nurse bring to the first shift?

Pens, a penlight, a stethoscope, your badge, and a brain sheet that fits your unit. Ask your preceptor which sheet the unit uses.

How do I organize 5 patients as a new nurse?

Use a multi-patient brain sheet with an hourly timeline, cluster care in each room, and update the timeline every hour.
