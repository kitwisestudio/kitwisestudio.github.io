The Be Ready checklist, item by item, with amounts

Work out your own kit in 1 minute: free Ready72 planner

On 22 October 2026 the Cabinet Office replaced the old Prepare website with Be Ready, the first stage of a three-year campaign. Its core message: be ready for 3 days, so you can manage while support reaches those most in need. The checklist tells you what to keep, but not how much. This page adds the amounts.

The 9 sections of the official checklist

SectionWhat the checklist saysFor 2 adults + 2 children
WaterBottled water for 3 days, 3 litres per person per day for drinking36 L (18 × 2 L bottles)
FoodNon-perishable food that needs no fridge or cooking, e.g. tinned meat, fish or vegetables, dried fruit and nuts24 tins, 4 packs of crackers, 4 bags of nuts, 2 boxes of cereal, 2 jars of peanut butter, long-life milk
HygieneHand sanitiser, wet wipes, hygiene and bathroom products2 sanitisers, 2 packs of wipes, 4 toilet rolls, bin bags
Light and warmthTorch and spare batteries; blankets, sleeping bags and warm clothing2 torches, 3 packs of batteries, 4 blankets or sleeping bags
CommunicationFM radio (battery or wind-up); charged power bank1 radio, about 40,000 mAh of power banks for 4 phones
Money and documentsCash in coins and notes; copies of IDs; written phone numbers and medication listAbout £80 cash, a zip bag of copies, one written list
MedicalFirst aid kit; essential medication and supplies1 kit; order repeats up to 5 working days early
BabyReady-to-feed formula; nappies and wipesAbout 2.7 L of ready-to-feed formula (0.9 L a day) and 30 nappies per baby
PetsPet food and bottled water3 days of food and about 4.5 L of water per dog

Amounts for food, batteries, power banks and cash are Ready72 estimates, not official figures. Only the 3 litres of water per person per day comes from the checklist.

What is new compared with the old Prepare list

The 3-litre water figure, cash, warm clothing, paper copies of documents and a written list of phone numbers and medication. The written list matters because a flat phone takes your contacts with it.

The 5 steps the government suggests

  1. See what you already have.
  2. Plan for your household, including medication, baby formula or pet food.
  3. Build it gradually during normal shopping.
  4. Check it every six months for expiry dates.
  5. Connect with your neighbours and swap contact details.

Questions

When did Be Ready launch?
The Cabinet Office launched it on 22 October 2026. The old Prepare site now redirects to beready.campaign.gov.uk.
Is the Be Ready checklist compulsory?
No. It is advice. Not everyone can take every step, and the government suggests doing what works for you.
Where is the official checklist?
At beready.campaign.gov.uk, where you can download it as a PDF. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own local advice on Ready Scotland and nidirect.
