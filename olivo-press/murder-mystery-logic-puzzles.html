Murder mystery logic puzzles: what they are and how to solve them

Updated 2026-10-28 · Olivo Press

A murder mystery logic puzzle is a grid on a floor plan where you place each suspect so that every row and every column holds exactly one person, following written clues. When the grid is complete, the killer is the only suspect left alone in a room with the victim. It is sudoku-style deduction with a story, and a good puzzle has exactly one solution.

The rules (Who Did It? style)

  1. The grid is a floor plan divided into rooms.
  2. Each row and each column holds exactly one person.
  3. Clues say where people are: "The chef was in the kitchen", "Nobody stood next to the window".
  4. Once everyone is placed, the murderer is the person alone in a room with the victim.

How to solve one

  1. Place the fixed clues first ("X was in the library").
  2. Cross out every other cell in that person's row and column.
  3. Use room-size clues: a room with one cell left in a row often forces a placement.
  4. Use negative clues to remove options, then repeat.
  5. Never guess: in a well-made puzzle the next move is always forced by logic.

Who they are for

People who like sudoku, logic grid puzzles, cozy mysteries or true crime and want something with a story. Warm-up 6x6 cases take minutes; 8x8 expert cases take much longer.

Quick answers

Are murder mystery logic puzzles like sudoku?

Yes. You place one item per row and column as in sudoku, but with suspects, rooms and written clues instead of digits.

Do these puzzles need guessing?

No. Each Who Did It? case is checked by a solver to have exactly one solution reachable by logic.

Is there a Spanish version?
