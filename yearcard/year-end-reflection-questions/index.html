40 year-end reflection questions to close 2026

A good year-end review takes 20 minutes and a few honest answers. Pick the questions that hit, write short answers, and keep them somewhere you'll see in January. If you want the quick version, YearCard asks 12 of these and turns your answers into story cards.

Looking back

  1. What is 2026 in one word?
  2. What was your best moment of the year?
  3. What surprised you most?
  4. Which song, show or book defined your year?
  5. What did you do for the first time?
  6. Where did you go that was new?
  7. Who did you meet that mattered?
  8. What made you laugh the hardest?

The hard parts

  1. What was the hardest thing you got through?
  2. What did you avoid that you need to face?
  3. When did you feel most like yourself?
  4. What drained your energy the most?
  5. What would you tell yourself on 1 January 2026?
  6. What are you ready to forgive?

Wins and lessons

  1. What was your biggest win?
  2. What is one thing 2026 taught you?
  3. Which habit helped you most?
  4. What are you proud of that nobody saw?
  5. What did you learn about money?
  6. What did you learn about the people around you?
  7. What skill did you improve?
  8. What risk paid off?

Where your energy went

  1. Where did most of your time go: work, family, love, health, travel, creating, going out or rest?
  2. Was that where you wanted it to go?
  3. Who got the best of you this year?
  4. What would you like to do less of?
  5. What would you like to do more of?
  6. How many new places, books, people and events did 2026 bring?

Into 2027

  1. What era are you entering: healing, money, glow-up, builder, adventure, love or discipline?
  2. What is one thing you want to be true by December 2027?
  3. What will you stop doing?
  4. What will you start this month, not in January?
  5. Who do you want to spend more time with?
  6. What does a good normal day look like for you?
  7. What are you looking forward to most?
  8. What is your 2027 word?

How to use these questions

Do it in one sitting, phone away, with something to write on. Short answers are fine. Read last year's answers if you have them. Then pick one action for next week, not a list of twenty. If you'd like to share your year, make your YearCard: it is free and nothing is uploaded.
