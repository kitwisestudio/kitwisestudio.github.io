OET Writing tips for nurses

Twelve practical rules, based on the six OET Writing criteria, that you can apply in your next practice letter.

Before you write (5 minutes reading time)

  1. Identify the reader and the purpose first. Read the task box before the notes: who is the reader, and what must they do?
  2. Mark each note as key, optional or not needed for that reader. A district nurse needs wound care and mobility; they do not need a resolved appendicectomy from 1978.
  3. Group notes by topic (condition, treatment, social situation, requests), not in the order they appear.

While you write (40 minutes)

  1. State the purpose in the first sentence: "I am writing to refer Mrs Lee, who requires…".
  2. Put the most urgent information early.
  3. Expand every note into full sentences. No bullet points, no "pt", "bd" or arrows.
  4. Use a formal register: no contractions, no "got better" or "a lot of".
  5. Keep the body around 180–200 words in 3–5 paragraphs.
  6. Make a clear request: "I would be grateful if you could…".
  7. Match the closing: "Dear Ms Doe" → "Yours sincerely"; "Dear Sir/Madam" → "Yours faithfully".

Before you finish

  1. Check names, dates and doses against the notes: a wrong dose changes the meaning.
  2. Re-read for typical errors: "since 3 days", "explain him", "informations" (see common mistakes).

Frequently asked questions

How much time should I spend planning an OET letter?

Use the 5 minutes of reading time to decide the purpose and select notes, then take about 5 more minutes at the start of the 40 minutes to plan paragraphs. Leave the last 3–5 minutes to check.
