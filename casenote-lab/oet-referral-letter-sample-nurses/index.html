OET referral letter sample for nurses (with case notes)

A referral letter asks another health professional to take over or add to a patient's care. Below is an original OET-style nursing case: a community nurse refers a patient with a non-healing venous leg ulcer to a tissue viability nurse.

How to structure an OET referral letter

  1. Purpose: who the patient is and what you want the reader to do (assess, review, advise).
  2. Relevant background: the diagnosis and the history that explains the request.
  3. Current situation: treatment so far, why it is not enough, risks (allergies, contraindications).
  4. Social factors that affect the plan.
  5. Request and close: what exactly you would like, and contact details.

Case notes: Non-healing leg ulcer – referral to tissue viability nurse

Writing task. Using the information in the case notes, write a letter of referral to Ms Fiona Grant, Tissue Viability Nurse Specialist at Lakeside Community Health Services, requesting specialist assessment of Mrs Mallory's leg ulcer. Address the letter to Ms Fiona Grant, Tissue Viability Nurse Specialist, Lakeside Community Health Services, Lakeside Clinic, 9 Marina Way, Lakeside. In your answer: expand the relevant notes into complete sentences; do not use note form; use letter format. The body of the letter should be approximately 180–200 words.

Model letter

Ms Fiona Grant Tissue Viability Nurse Specialist Lakeside Community Health Services Lakeside Clinic 9 Marina Way Lakeside 12 March 2026 Dear Ms Grant, Re: Mrs Irene Mallory, DOB 21/09/1947 I am writing to refer Mrs Mallory for specialist assessment of a venous leg ulcer on her left medial gaiter area, which has deteriorated despite 10 weeks of treatment. The ulcer has increased in size to 6.5 x 4cm. The wound bed is 60% slough and 40% granulation tissue. Heavy serous exudate causes daily strike-through, and the surrounding skin is macerated. It is currently dressed with Aquacel Extra hydrofiber and an absorbent pad three times weekly. Mrs Mallory reports pain of 6/10 at dressing changes despite taking paracetamol 1g four times daily. Her ABPI was 0.78 in January, and reduced compression bandaging has been applied since 1 February, which she tolerates. She has a history of a left leg DVT in 2009 and also has type 2 diabetes. Please note that she is allergic to Elastoplast. Because of pain, she sleeps in an armchair at night, so her legs remain dependent. I would be grateful if you could review her dressing and compression regimen and consider whether a vascular referral is required. Please do not hesitate to contact me if you require further information. Yours sincerely, Community Staff Nurse Lakeside District Nursing Team

Why the model letter works

  • The first sentence states the request (specialist assessment) and the main problem (a deteriorating ulcer despite 10 weeks of treatment).
  • The wound is described in a logical order: size, wound bed, exudate and skin, current dressing, pain.
  • Vascular status (ABPI) is placed next to the compression details because the TVN will use them together to decide treatment.
  • The Elastoplast allergy and sleeping in a chair are short notes but essential: they affect dressing choice and oedema.
  • Old unrelated surgery, charity work and the DN visiting days are omitted. The cause (car door) is optional background.
  • The final paragraph repeats the request with specific actions, which helps the reader know exactly what is expected.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a referral and a discharge letter in OET?

A referral asks the reader to start or add care (for example a specialist assessment). A discharge letter hands over care when the patient leaves hospital and focuses on what must continue at home.
