How to structure an OET discharge letter

  1. Purpose: the patient is being discharged today and what you need the reader to do.
  2. Admission and treatment: briefly, only what matters for ongoing care.
  3. Current condition: wound, pain, mobility, medicines on discharge.
  4. Follow-up: appointments, removal of sutures, warning signs.
  5. Request and close.

Case notes: Child after appendicectomy – wound review

Writing task. Using the information in the case notes, write a letter to Ms Fiona Grant, Practice Nurse at Leo's GP surgery, who will review his wound and remove his sutures. Address the letter to Ms Fiona Grant, Practice Nurse, Hawthorn Medical Practice, 22 Mill Lane, Kettering NN15 6TQ. In your answer: expand the relevant notes into complete sentences; do not use note form; use letter format. The body of the letter should be approximately 180–200 words.

Model letter

Ms Fiona Grant Practice Nurse Hawthorn Medical Practice 22 Mill Lane Kettering NN15 6TQ 12 January 2026 Dear Ms Grant, Re: Leo Dawson, DOB 03/05/2017 I am writing to request that you review the umbilical wound of Leo Dawson, an eight-year-old boy who is being discharged home today, and remove his sutures on 19 January. Leo was admitted on 8 January with acute appendicitis and underwent a laparoscopic appendicectomy the following day. The appendix was found to be gangrenous but had not perforated. He received intravenous antibiotics for 48 hours and is now taking oral co-amoxiclav 250/62 mg suspension, 5 mL three times daily, for a further five days. His pain is well controlled with regular paracetamol and ibuprofen, which may be given as required after 48 hours. Although he has mild asthma, he has tolerated ibuprofen without any problems. Two port sites closed with tissue glue are healing well. However, the umbilical wound, which has non-absorbable sutures, shows mild erythema without discharge. A wound swab was taken yesterday and the result is pending. His parents have been taught to recognise the signs of wound infection. Leo should stay off school for one week and avoid contact sports and physical education for four weeks. Please do not hesitate to contact me if you have any queries. Yours sincerely, Staff Nurse Ward 12 (Paediatric Surgery), Northfield General Hospital

Why the model letter works

  • The opening sentence tells the practice nurse exactly what is needed (wound review and suture removal) and when.
  • The presenting symptoms, admission blood results and needle anxiety are left out: they are history, and the practice nurse does not need them to check a wound.
  • Asthma is a tricky note: it is only worth mentioning because it shows that ibuprofen is safe for Leo.
  • The wound details are grouped together and the problem wound (umbilical erythema, swab pending) comes after the ones that are healing well, introduced with 'However'.
  • Useful phrases: 'underwent a laparoscopic appendicectomy', 'may be given as required', 'the result is pending'.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include the full hospital history in a discharge letter?

No. Include only what the reader needs to continue care after discharge: current condition, medicines, follow-up and warning signs.
