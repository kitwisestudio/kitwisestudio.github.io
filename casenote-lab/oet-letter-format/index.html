OET letter format for nurses

Organisation & Layout is one of the six OET Writing criteria. A clear, standard format helps the reader find information quickly.

Ms Jane Doe District Nurse Northfield Community Health Centre 12 Mill Road, Northfield 14 March 2026 Dear Ms Doe, Re: Mr Arthur Bell, DOB 03/02/1954 [Paragraph 1 – purpose: who the patient is and what you want the reader to do] [Paragraph 2 – relevant background and the reason for admission] [Paragraph 3 – treatment, current condition and social situation] [Paragraph 4 – specific requests and follow-up] Yours sincerely, Charge Nurse Ward 7, Riverside General Hospital

Rules that cost marks

  • Salutation and closing must match: "Dear Ms Doe" → "Yours sincerely"; "Dear Sir/Madam" → "Yours faithfully".
  • Use title and surname only: "Dear Ms Doe", not "Dear Ms Jane Doe" or "Dear Jane".
  • State the purpose in the first sentence: "I am writing to refer Mr Bell, who requires…".
  • No note form: expand notes into complete sentences, and write abbreviations such as "bd" or "PRN" in full.
  • No contractions or informal words: "does not", not "doesn't"; "improved", not "got better".
  • Body length: about 180–200 words, in 3–5 paragraphs.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to write the address in OET Writing?

Write the recipient's name, role and address as given in the task, followed by the date. You do not need to write your own address.

Should I write "Re:" in an OET letter?

A Re: line with the patient's name (and date of birth if given) is a common, clear way to show who the letter is about.
