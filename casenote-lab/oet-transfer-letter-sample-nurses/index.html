OET transfer letter sample for nurses (with case notes)

A transfer letter hands a patient over to another unit or facility, such as a rehabilitation unit or nursing home. Below is an original OET-style case: a stroke patient is transferred to a rehabilitation unit.

How to structure an OET transfer letter

  1. Purpose: the patient is being transferred today and why.
  2. Diagnosis and course: what happened and what has been done.
  3. Current care needs: mobility, transfers, swallowing and diet, continence, skin, communication.
  4. Risks: falls, aspiration, pressure areas.
  5. Goals and request: what the receiving team should continue.

Case notes: Stroke – transfer to rehabilitation unit

Writing task. Using the information in the case notes, write a letter to Ms Niamh Doyle, Clinical Nurse Manager at the Rehabilitation Unit, Ardmore Rehabilitation Centre, where Mr Quinlan is being transferred today for ongoing rehabilitation. Address the letter to Ms Niamh Doyle, Clinical Nurse Manager, Rehabilitation Unit, Ardmore Rehabilitation Centre, Ardmore Road, Limerick. In your answer: expand the relevant notes into complete sentences; do not use note form; use letter format. The body of the letter should be approximately 180–200 words.

Model letter

Ms Niamh Doyle Clinical Nurse Manager Rehabilitation Unit Ardmore Rehabilitation Centre Ardmore Road Limerick 5 March 2026 Dear Ms Doyle, Re: Mr Patrick Quinlan, DOB 12/04/1958 I am writing to transfer Mr Quinlan, who had a left middle cerebral artery ischaemic stroke on 18 February, to your unit for ongoing rehabilitation. His goals are to improve his mobility and communication and to return home. Mr Quinlan has right-sided weakness (arm 2/5, leg 3/5) and requires the assistance of two staff and a standing aid for transfers. He is at high risk of falls, so a sensor alarm is in use. He also has expressive dysphasia; however, he understands simple commands and communicates using yes/no answers and a picture board. Following speech and language therapy assessment, he requires a minced and moist diet (IDDSI level 5) and mildly thick fluids (level 2). His medications include clopidogrel 75mg, atorvastatin 80mg and ramipril 2.5mg daily. His urinary catheter was removed on 25 February, and he is now incontinent at night, for which pads are used. His Waterlow score is 18, so he requires two-hourly repositioning; his sacrum is currently intact. He has been tearful at times, and a mood screen is pending. His wife visits daily and wishes to be involved in his care. Please contact me if you require any further information. Yours sincerely, Staff Nurse Acute Stroke Unit Shannonside University Hospital

Why the model letter works

  • The opening gives the diagnosis, the reason for transfer and the patient's rehabilitation goals, so the reader immediately understands the aim of care.
  • Information is grouped by care need: mobility and falls, communication, swallowing and medication, continence and skin, then mood and family.
  • The normal follow-up CT scan is a tricky distractor – it reassures but does not change rehabilitation nursing.
  • Farming background, hobbies, insurance, reading glasses and old hernia repair are not needed by the rehabilitation team.
  • Specific details (IDDSI levels, Waterlow score, two-staff transfers) are kept exact because the receiving nurses must act on them safely.
  • Useful phrases: 'requires the assistance of two staff', 'for which pads are used', 'wishes to be involved in his care'.

Frequently asked questions

How do I order information in an OET transfer letter?

Start with the purpose, then the diagnosis, then current care needs grouped by topic (mobility, nutrition, continence), and finish with the request. Put urgent risks early.
