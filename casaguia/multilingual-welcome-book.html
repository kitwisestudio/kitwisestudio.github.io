How do I make a welcome book for my holiday rental in several languages?

Updated 2026-11-08 · CasaGuía

Write the content once, then add translations of the standard parts (house rules, check-out, recycling, emergency numbers) and keep your own notes short, so there is less to translate. CasaGuía does this automatically for English, Spanish, German and French and prints an A5 or A4 PDF.

Why it matters

In the Balearic Islands there were 21,304 registered tourist homes in May 2026 (INE experimental statistics). Many guests in Spain come from abroad, so a book in only one language leaves some of them out.

What to include in every language

  1. Welcome and host contact.
  2. Wi-Fi with a QR code (how to make one).
  3. Check-in and check-out times, how to get in, parking.
  4. House rules (ready-made in 4 languages).
  5. Appliances, rubbish and recycling.
  6. Emergency numbers: 112 in Spain and across the EU.
  7. Local recommendations and a check-out checklist.

Tips

  • Translate fixed texts once and reuse them; write personal tips in plain, short sentences.
  • Use one language per page or per column so guests find theirs fast.

Quick answers

Which languages should a holiday rental welcome book be in?

The languages of your usual guests. On the Spanish coast and islands that is usually English, Spanish, German and French.

Do I have to translate everything?

No. Translate the standard parts (rules, check-out, recycling, emergencies) and keep your own notes short.
