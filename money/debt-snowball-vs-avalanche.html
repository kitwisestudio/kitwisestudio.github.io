Debt snowball vs avalanche: which one saves more?

Updated 2026-10-29 · KitWise Money Math

In our example (4 debts, $21,000, minimums of $530 a month plus $200 extra), the avalanche was debt-free in 35 months with $4,543 of interest and the snowball in 36 months with $4,728: only about $185 apart. When rates are far apart the gap grows (about $1,460 in a second example). But the biggest lever is the monthly amount: paying $730 instead of $530 cut interest from $9,749 to $4,543.

When the gap gets bigger

With a $3,000 student loan at 4.5% and a $12,000 card at 27.9% ($620 a month), the avalanche paid $5,066 in interest and the snowball $6,526, about $1,460 more.

Which to choose

  • Rates far apart: the avalanche (highest rate first) saves real money.
  • Rates similar, or you have quit before: the snowball's quick wins cost little.
  • Either way, raise the monthly payment: it beats any ordering.

Quick answers

Is the debt avalanche better than the snowball?

Mathematically yes, but often by little. In our $21,000 example the avalanche saved about $185 and one month; with very different rates it saved about $1,460.

What matters most when paying off debt?

The monthly amount. Going from $530 to $730 a month cut interest from $9,749 to $4,543 and finished 24 months sooner.
