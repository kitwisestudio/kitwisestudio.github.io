The rule of 72, checked against the exact math

Updated 2026-10-29 · KitWise Money Math

The rule of 72 estimates how many years it takes money to double: 72 ÷ yearly rate. At 7%, 72 ÷ 7 = 10.3 years (the exact answer is 10.2). At 10%, about 7.2 years. It works against you too: at 3% inflation, cash loses half its buying power in about 24 years (exact: 23.4).

Rule vs exact

RateRule of 72Exact (ln 2 ÷ ln(1+r))
3%24.0 years23.4
7%10.3 years10.2
10%7.2 years7.3

$10,000 at an assumed 7% becomes about $20,000 in 10 years, $40,000 in 20 and $80,000 in 30: each doubling is bigger than the last.
